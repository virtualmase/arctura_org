Autonomous Resource Management

Authority before action.

Autonomous Resource Management, or ARMS, is Arctura’s proposed control plane for agents that need to operate against real resources. It binds identity, mandate, permissions, approvals, execution context, and audit receipts into one inspectable record.

Design rule

An agent may act only within an explicit mandate.

ARMS is conceptual. It is not a custodial service, trading system, medical tool, legal tool, or a license for unbounded autonomous behavior.

Safeguard: The objective is controlled delegation, not the removal of accountability. High-impact actions require defined policy, approval, auditability, and an escalation path to a responsible human.

Control stack

Identity, policy, resources, execution, memory.

The visual layer is simple; the operating structure must be precise. Each action should answer who acted, under which mandate, against what resource, with what permission, what happened, and how it can be reviewed.

01identity

Agent identity

Every agent has a disclosed identifier, operator, purpose, origin, and status. Identity is not personality; it is accountability.

Who is acting?
02mandate

Mandate

A mandate defines purpose, allowed actions, prohibited actions, resource limits, duration, review interval, and escalation owner.

What may it do?
03policy

Permissions

Permissions connect the mandate to particular APIs, datasets, compute budgets, wallets, services, and write scopes.

Where may it act?
04approval

Approval gate

Actions are classified by risk. Some execute automatically inside policy; others pause for human approval or multi-party review.

Who must agree?
05receipt

Execution receipt

Each action creates a timestamped receipt containing intent, inputs, policy reference, output, cost, state, and reviewer path.

What happened?
06review

Audit and revocation

Operators can inspect history, revoke access, rotate permissions, set expiration, and learn from exceptions without rewriting the past.
