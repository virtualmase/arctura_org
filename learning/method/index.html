Learning method

Understanding should change the work.

ARCTURA learning moves through four stages: understand a concept, examine a case, make an artifact, and improve it through review. The artifact is where explanation becomes accountable practice.

Assessment posture

Show the reasoning.

We do not treat fluency, confidence, or polished prose as proof of understanding. A learner should be able to expose choices, assumptions, evidence, and limits.

The cycle

Four stages, one inspectable outcome.

The cycle can fit a 30-minute exercise or a multi-week project. What changes is the depth of evidence and review—not the basic discipline.

Concept

Learn the vocabulary, model, history, and boundary. Explain the idea in plain language and distinguish it from nearby ideas.

Case

Examine a bounded situation. Separate observations from interpretations and compare more than one plausible reading.

Artifact

Apply the concept to real structure: a decision record, domain model, review cycle, evidence note, or other object.

Review

Invite a specific challenge. Record what changed, what remains unresolved, and what should trigger another review.

Review rubric

Ask six questions before calling the work ready.

Dimension Review question Evidence of strength
Purpose Is the question bounded and worth answering? The artifact names a decision, audience, use, and excluded scope.
Structure Does the model fit the problem? Important entities, options, roles, relationships, or stages are explicit.
Evidence Can claims be traced and challenged? Sources are identifiable; observation, interpretation, and assumption are distinct.
Alternatives What else could be true or done? The artifact includes competing options, counterexamples, or stakeholder views.
Boundary What does this work not establish? Uncertainty, missing evidence, permissions, and professional limits travel with it.
Revision What would reopen the work? An owner, next action, review date, or observable trigger is named.

Review protocol

Critique the artifact, not the person.

A useful review identifies the exact element under challenge, explains why it matters, and proposes evidence or a test. “I disagree” is a starting signal, not a complete review.

Describe

Restate the artifact's purpose and strongest claim before challenging it. Confirm that you are reviewing the intended work.

Establish shared context

Challenge

Name one unsupported assumption, omitted alternative, ambiguous term, weak handoff, or boundary that needs attention.

Make critique actionable

Revise

Record the response: accepted change, rejected change with rationale, or unresolved question with a next test.

Preserve the decision trail

Worked review

Turn a vague objection into an actionable challenge.

Suppose a reviewer says, “This model is too simple.” A useful response asks which required question the model cannot answer. The reviewer identifies that the model records a decision and its evidence but cannot represent when a cited source was superseded. The learner adds source lifecycle state and a replacement relationship, updates a sample record, and reruns the competency question.

The revision note preserves the original gap, the specific challenge, the accepted change, and the test that now passes. If the learner rejects the change, the record should explain why the detail is outside the model's stated purpose. Either outcome is more useful than resolving the exchange through confidence or preference.

Use and limits

Practice can improve judgment; it cannot confer authority.

These materials support independent learning and peer review. They do not certify competence, replace supervised practice, or authorize engineering, legal, medical, financial, security, or other professional decisions. Match review depth to consequence.
